When we're kids, everything is new and big and different. As we get older, it can feel like nothing is novel anymore. I was listening to Robert Greene, the author of *The 48 Laws of Power*, talk about this, and it left me with a question: how do you maintain that childhood-like curiosity into adulthood?

That's what I want to explore in **The Small Mind and the Big Mind**. How do you keep that sense of wonder as more of the world starts to feel familiar?

## Notes for writing

- Start with the contrast between childhood discovery and adult familiarity.
- Explore what the “small mind” and the “big mind” mean in the conversation before applying them to my own life.
- Add a personal example of something that once felt new, and another moment when that curiosity came back.
- Return to the central question: how do you maintain childhood-like curiosity into adulthood?
