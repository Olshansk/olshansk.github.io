Whenever someone sends me a “long” (more than 5 minute voice note), they usually end with an apology. My usual response is: “Don’t apologize.”

No one ever asks for an apology when scheduling a 30 minute call without an agenda. No one apologizes if a meeting goes 5 minutes over. No one apologizes for sending a hello without context (see [nohello.net](https://nohello.net)). No one apologizes for sending text messages without having fully thought through why they need your attention.

My opinion on this is clear and simple: **We need to normalize long voice notes as a tool in our communication toolbox.**

I’ve found that the tools social media platforms give us end up driving how we communicate. The basic ones are obviously text, video and audio chats, but it gets more nuanced. Use cases for voice notes:

  1. **Friends on the other side of the world** \- You’re schedule is so misaligned with a friend on the other side of the world that you’ve failed to schedule time for weeks or months.

  2. **Ongoing conversations** \- Complex strategic discussions you have with your partners at work.

  3. **On the go** \- There’s a lot of context you need to share with someone
