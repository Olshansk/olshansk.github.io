I can't recall the last day I spent from start to finish without talking about AI. In some ways it reminds me of COVID.

There's a key difference though. We all knew COVID was a battle we had to get through. AI is a tool that's accelerating at an exponential pace.

I've had a handful of conversations where people ask me for my opinion on AI. I wanted to capture some of my meta level thoughts on the topic.

What do I want AI to be:

1. Maximally **useful**
2. Maximally **abundant**
3. Maximally **reliable**
4. Maximally **safe**

All in equal parts.

I don't want to get into the tactics, but I do want to get into a few examples that are important to me.

Why does this matter?

- What do I want: achieve my full potential and for everyone around me to do the same.

## Education

I've long held the belief that the education system needs a revamp. It's not a novel thought, nor do I have a concrete idea right now. It's more of a vibe I like to discuss with friends.

In the past, I would think of how we leverage mental plasticity of kids to accelerate learning in subjects where they show interests and capability, while
still create an environment of equality and fun.

With AI, it seems like we'll have specialized tutors that grow with us.

This is a very challenging problem, but one that I'm hopeful for.

## Traditional industries

Through my parents and my sister, I've been exposed to the construction (geotech & civil) industries as well as speech therapy.

Both of these industries are still in the "ChatGPT drafted in email for me" phase that most software engineers were in early 2023.

I believe there are two key reasons for this:

1. People in tech (PMs, Engineers, Leaders, Entrepreneurs, etc) thrive on growth, novelty and change. We're always looking for the next thing.
2. The frontier models are being pre/post trained to learn from, which leads to capitalize on the above.

Software engineering on the other hand has had access to sophisticated tooling for decades.
