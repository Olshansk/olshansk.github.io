I recently spent quite a bit of time with people outside the "tech bubble".

Some are software engineers but are slightly older and work in banks where things move more slowly. Others are completely outside the tech industry.

The range of perspectives and emotions toward AI spans a very broad spectrum. Some hate it, some are afraid of it, some don't care, and others are excited about it.

Unsurprisingly, no one I spoke to is a power user. It's hard when you're on X, always testing the latest and greatest tools. However, everyone called out that AI makes mistakes, hallucinates, and is unreliable in different ways.

I have a strong conviction that the gateway to AI will continue to become more reliable. Be it through the foundation model, the harness, the data, or other layers, I don't know whether any individual's definition of "perfect" will ever be achieved.

To explain this, I thought of an analogy on the spot, and it resonated:

> Artificial Intelligence and Organic Intelligence have their own pros and cons. Neither is nor will be Perfect Intelligence.

While this didn't solve any problems, it grounded people's expectations.

The conversation took a different direction from this point on.
