## Your greatest strength can also be your greatest weakness

- When asked about his management weaknesses, Jobs starts with:

  > I don’t know.

- Then he says:

  > People are package deals; you take the good with the confused.

  — Steve Jobs, interview collected in [*Make Something Wonderful*](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), also discussed in my [00:04 snip](https://share.snipd.com/snip/cc64a501-7061-4676-bb8c-7b9a216c6c0c).

- I think that says a lot about someone's greatest strength also being their greatest weakness. The same trait can help you in one situation and hurt you in another.
- One useful wrinkle from the full interview: Jobs eventually does identify a weakness. He describes being too idealistic and sometimes pursuing the best possible outcome instead of a practical improvement. That connects directly to the NeXT story.

## A stable home can make room for creative intensity

- Wilson talks about Jobs finding a stable family life with Laurene Powell, and brings up Bach as another example of a creative person with a family life. [Saved snip, 25:56](https://share.snipd.com/snip/ef9c0c20-88ca-4d73-90a5-979aeb72219d).
- The thought that stood out to me: maybe stability at home lets you channel more of your creativity and anxiety into your work and the rest of your life.
- I want to explore that relationship more. It's an interesting lens for thinking about creators and artists, rather than a rule that explains everyone's life.

## NeXT was where he learned to make trade-offs

- I associate Jobs with keeping the iPhone simple and cutting departments, projects, and complexity. It's interesting to contrast that with NeXT, where he seemed to want to fit everything into one computer.
- In my [16:26 snip](https://share.snipd.com/snip/408cc904-a6d5-44cd-8c64-52ac1df299dc), Wilson describes the team trying to balance price, timing, and capabilities. Jobs wanted every priority satisfied. The project exceeded its intended budget and deadline.
- My takeaway: early Apple success involved youth, timing, and some luck. Success didn't mean he had already learned every lesson a founder needs. NeXT was where he made many of those mistakes, and he returned to Apple as a more experienced founder.
- The [Steve Jobs Archive](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/) describes that later focus: after returning, he reduced Apple's product offerings from seventeen to four.
- I also hadn't realized there was a roughly $10,000 computer in this story. The 1988 NeXT launched at $6,500; an optional hard drive and laser printer brought it to $10,500. [Contemporary reporting](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/business/1988/10/13/amid-hoopla-next-computer-is-unveiled-by-pc-pioneer-jobs/7786847b-539b-49b1-9771-fea07df76407/).
