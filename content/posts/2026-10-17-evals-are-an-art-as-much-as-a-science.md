Evals are an art as much as a science.

There's no such thing as off-the-shelf software for every purpose, and there never will be off-the-shelf evals for every purpose either.

People think you can hand someone an eval framework. But the reason there are so many FDEs is that you need eval deployment. There's a very big difference between giving someone an SDK, a framework, or a toolkit and deploying it and doing the work with them.

You give someone tools, and some people can take them and run with them on their own. But eval is more of a process.

The final product of an eval framework is a verb: **eval**. The final product of a software product is **software**. It's a noun. It's not code.

That's why giving someone an eval framework and doing evals with them are two very different things.
