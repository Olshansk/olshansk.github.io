Reading [Gary Lin’s post on incremental change](https://x.com/garymlin/status/2113296914339049603) and [OpenAI’s Forward Deployed Engineering page](https://openai.com/business/forward-deployed-engineering/).

> Slow is smooth and smooth is fast.

— Gary Lin

A lot of enterprises are seeking an answer to how to get value from integrating AI into their workflows.

There’s no single silver bullet out of the box.

In the same way that the technology needs to be adapted to a particular use case, so does the process.

Understanding requirements, constraints, goals, and data sources are some well-established concepts in the industry.

More so, understanding the workflows, their integrations, their context, their history, and the people behind them is what’s needed today more than ever before.

The FDE org at OAI strikes a very delicate balance between sharing reusable learnings among its members, building on top of what the research and product orgs have developed, and also customizing a solution to an organization’s particular needs.

And if you want to learn more about the concrete offerings today, see [OpenAI’s page](https://openai.com/business/forward-deployed-engineering/).

I’ve heard someone describe Enterprise Context as something that has “Problem Market Fit.”

No matter how much ink has been spilled on context engineering, this is still an unsolved problem.
