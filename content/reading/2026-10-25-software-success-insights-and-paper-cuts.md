Reading notes and reflections by Daniel Olshansky.

Source: [AI is driving a boom in modestly successful software projects](https://seangoedecke.com/ai-is-driving-a-boom-in-modestly-successful-software/) — Sean Goedecke.

> but I think it’s still enough to conclude that AI is indeed driving a boom in hobbyist software.

> It’s less clear to me why there aren’t many new wildly successful hobbyist projects. Maybe it’s just hard to have a really good idea for a product, and AI can’t help with that. Or maybe it takes years for a really successful project to develop, and we need to be patient for the current crop of thousand-user tools to grow into hundred-thousand-user tools.

A few years ago, I started working on a blog post titled “Public Source Is Not Open Source.” This post talked a lot about the explosion of add-ons and plugins.

It made me reflect that a successful software product is just one insight, from one or a few individuals, followed by years of hard work and add-ons.

Think about Google: Larry and Sergey had the insight of PageRank, and then Google did the rest. Arguably, Jeff Dean and Sundar Pichai did a lot more for Google than the others. However, we remember the founders.

What I’m getting at is that solving the thousands of paper cuts and little things is what makes a product widely adopted.

On the other side, I’ve also seen teams focused on all the little things before the big thing worked. You need both, in sequence, at the right time, not just one or the other.

Maybe open source will finally have its renaissance as both humans and agents add what they need to existing products.
