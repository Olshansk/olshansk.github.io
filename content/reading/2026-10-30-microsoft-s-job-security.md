A couple of things stood out to me in this summary of Microsoft's voluntary exit offer.

First, the package values tenure differently depending on level. The screenshot says employees at level 64 and below receive one week of pay for every six months of service, while those at levels 65–67 receive two weeks. That is a meaningful difference in how their time is valued.

For someone who has worked there for ten years, that formula gives 20 weeks at the lower rate and 40 weeks at the higher rate—about nine months, rather than a full year, before any cap applies. That seems reasonable to me.

Continued vesting for six to twelve months also sounds reasonable to me, though I still need to verify that duration for this particular offer.

Even more interesting are the teams. Everyone talks about job security, but which teams participate in an exit program seems worth paying attention to. The screenshot lists Copilot, AI Experiences, Xbox, Windows + Devices, HR, and Finance as included; most data center operations, core commercial sales, marketing, and cybersecurity as excluded.

What does that tell us about where Microsoft wants to retain people, and where it is making room for change?

### Source and verification notes

These screenshots are a Google AI Overview, saved here so I can trace the original sources later. Their detailed team list and payout terms still need checking against the actual offer documents. Eligibility for a voluntary exit offer does not, by itself, establish whether a team is safe from layoffs.

[Todd Bishop's October 26 report in GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/microsoft-makes-new-voluntary-exit-offer-to-more-than-a-quarter-of-its-workforce-including-some-execs/) confirms a voluntary offer for participating teams, with pay based on tenure and level and continued stock vesting. It does not specify the exact vesting duration or the full payout formula.
