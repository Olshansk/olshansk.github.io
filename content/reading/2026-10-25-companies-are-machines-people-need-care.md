Reading notes and reflections by Daniel Olshansky.

Source: [Don't anthropomorphize big tech companies](https://seangoedecke.com/dont-anthropomorphize-big-tech-companies/) — Sean Goedecke.

Pretty good advice. ChatGPT can give great advice, but it doesn’t care about you in the same way a friend does. Similarly, friends appreciate effort, but machines don’t. Similarly, organizations appreciate results, but you need to show care for the people.

> But if you treat your company like a machine, this shouldn’t be surprising at all.

> It’s hard to predict high-impact work, but it’s not impossible. The best way to operate at a big tech company — as I’ve said again andagain — is to try and develop this skill, and to spend most of your time looking for the low-effort high-reward wins. It’s hard to do that if you’re secretly imagining your company as a person.

And man, organizations are scary:

> No relationship with an individual person at your company will save you from being careless with the machine: it will chew you up and spit you out. You need to learn how to drive the car. This is scary, because it means that sometimes you will just get punished for no reason. But it’s also good news, because it means that you can get disproportionate rewards for relatively little effort, so long as you make the right decisions.
