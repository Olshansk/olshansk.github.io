Reading notes and reflections by Daniel Olshansky.

Source: [Software's centaur age may last decades](https://seangoedecke.com/softwares-centaur-age-may-last-decades/) — Sean Goedecke.

I like how he said that hand knitting saw its light for 200 years, while those like chess only lasted 20 years.

<!-- Review before publication: the source compares human-and-machine partnerships in knitting and chess, not unaided hand knitting. Original wording retained pending author review. -->

This quote hits a lot of points, both in terms of partnerships with humans and with agents:

> Lean into the partnership. A guaranteed way to fail in a centaur age is to refuse to become a centaur. The idea that AI is a passing fad was a reasonable position to hold in 2023, but we’re way past that now. The AI financial bubble popping will not mean the end of AI. It’s here to stay.

> In 2023, technical expertise was the primary source of human value. Today, I personally think it’s going to come down to alignment, but this is still early days. I’ve also seen arguments for taste, though it’s hard to define that non-circularly.

Everyone looks to AI to seek alignment. In reality, alignment is the value proposition that remains with us.
