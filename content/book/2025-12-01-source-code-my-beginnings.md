## Intro <!-- omit in toc -->

This is one of my favorite books over the last few years, and one that I can see myself recommending to others for years to come.

Bill Gates separated from Microsoft years ago and barely owns any stock in the company, but he's still a household name known for co-founding Microsoft.

This book is very much the origin story of Bill. It has vibes similar to the very first Iron Man film, but is grounded in non-fiction and childhood.

Even though we grew up under completely different circumstances, at different times, in different countries, around different religions, and with different household incomes, so much of what he said resonated with me. I guess nature—having ADHD and being somewhere on the spectrum—does end up being a key driver in what you do throughout childhood, regardless of where nurture takes you.

Bill grew up in an affluent family in the US, around Christianity, in the 1960s/1970s. I grew up in a household with limited resources, immigrating multiple times, in a Jewish community, in the 1990s/2000s. Both of us found books, games, and hobbies to obsess over. We had friends with whom we would dream, outdoor excursions that shaped our experiences, and friendships that came and went. Very different, but familiar throughout.

**Living in Seattle as I read this made me think how cool it'd be to go on a hike together. Bill, if you're reading this, it's an open invitation :)**

## Recurring moments  <!-- omit in toc -->

I'm writing this review 6–12 months after reading the book. After such a long time, you forget the details, but you remember the vibe.

**Bill's friend (Ken)**: Bill wrote at length about his friend who had aspirations bigger than his. Ken would carry a briefcase. They'd share business ideas. They'd go on hikes together. Ken was one of those people who would push himself to the limit in every aspect of life, and it's depressing that he tragically passed away in a hiking accident in his teens. His memory lived on through Bill and inspired me to keep pushing as I read the book in my 30s.

**Bill's partner (Allan)**: Everyone knows about Allan, but he always felt like a shadow. It was cool to see light shed on his personality as Bill's older friend who introduced him to new experiences, drugs, and ideas and, over time, became Bill's technical right-hand man. In particular, I love the passage where Allan wrote code by hand on a plane, and it successfully compiled, running on the second attempt during a live demo at one of Microsoft's most critical turning points.

**Bill's mom**: As someone who is very close to his mom and very grateful for everything his mom has sacrificed and done for him, I have a lot of respect for Bill's mom. She's a woman I would have loved to meet. She knew the sort of family she wanted to have and the community she wanted to build, and she made it happen. I could tell she was strict, caring, and supportive in equal parts, which is not an easy balance.
